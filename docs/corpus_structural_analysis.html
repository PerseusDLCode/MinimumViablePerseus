Structural Analysis of the Perseus Corpora

Generated 2026-05-23  ·  3,774 documents across four corpora  ·  Analysis by automated audit + classification pipeline

1. Summary

3,774 TEI-encoded documents from four corpora (canonical-greekLit, first1kgreek, csel, canonical-latinLit) were audited and classified by structural encoding pattern. Six structural families were identified. Five families correspond to distinct migration strategies; the sixth (97 documents, 2.6%) could not be classified and requires manual inspection.

The dominant family — hierarchical <div> structure (family 1) — accounts for 53% of the corpus. Milestone-primary citation (family 3) is the second-largest at 18.5%, concentrated heavily in canonical-latinLit. Drama (family 4) is a small but structurally distinctive minority. Approximately 300 documents across families 3 and 6 carry no effective citation declarations and cannot be migrated automatically.

2. Method

Each document was processed by two auditors: StructureAuditor, which analyzes the <div> hierarchy, milestone inventory, and citable element types; and ReferenceAuditor, which analyzes the <refsDecl> and <citeStructure> elements in the <teiHeader>. Output is a JSON report per document.

The classifier (classify_corpus.py) assigns each report to a structural family based on four signals: structural_type (from StructureAuditor), leaf element type (the element at maximum citation depth), milestone unit names, and div subtype vocabulary. Classification is deterministic and rule-based; no machine learning is involved. Family assignment is ordered by specificity: family 5 (parallel tradition) is tested before family 3 (milestone-primary) so that texts with both div structure and scholarly milestones are not misclassified as purely milestone-driven.

The corpora audited were: canonical-greekLit (1,612 documents), first1kgreek (1,204 documents), csel (271 documents), and canonical-latinLit (687 documents). Forty-five latinLit documents that failed XML parsing are excluded from all counts.

3. Structural Families

The families are defined by how citable units are encoded in the TEI source, not by literary genre. A text's family determines which migration stylesheet(s) are required and what <citeStructure> shape is appropriate.

Family 1 — Hierarchical div 2,002 documents  ·  53.0% of corpus

All citable units are <div type="textpart"> elements. The citation hierarchy is expressed by nesting depth and the @subtype attribute (book, chapter, section, etc.). The leaf citation unit is a <div>, <p>, or <ab> — never a <l> or <milestone>.

This family maps cleanly onto <citeStructure>: each nesting level becomes one <citeStructure> child, using match="tei:div[@subtype='book']" (or equivalent) and use="@n". The existing transform1.xsl handles the book/chapter/section shape, which alone covers the majority of these documents.

Most common cRefPattern shapes:

  • section(796)
  • chapter(353)
  • section / chapter(220)
  • verse / chapter(117)
  • chapter / book(90)
  • section / chapter / book(68)
  • fragment(61)
  • Jebb_page(42)
  • section / book(31)
  • paragraph(21)
The 42 documents classified under the pattern Jebb_page are likely misclassified. Jebb pages are a scholarly parallel citation tradition (analogous to Bekker or Stephanus) and those texts belong in family 5. The classifier should add Jebb_page to the PARALLEL_TRADITION_MILESTONES vocabulary.
Family 2 — Verse with <l> leaf 611 documents  ·  16.2% of corpus

The upper citation levels (book, poem, chapter) are encoded as <div type="textpart"> elements, but the citable leaf is a <l> element carrying an @n attribute. Examples include Homer (book / line), the Anthologia Palatina (book / epigram / line), and Latin verse texts.

The cRefPattern shapes for this family frequently name only the upper-level div units (chapter, book, section) without explicitly naming the line level. This is a known encoding pattern in the Perseus corpus: the <l> is the structural leaf but the refsDecl often omits it. Any generated <citeStructure> must therefore include an explicit <citeStructure unit="line" match="tei:l" use="@n"/> level, derived from document inspection rather than from the cref patterns alone.

Most common cRefPattern shapes:

  • chapter(94)
  • section / chapter(81)
  • section(76)
  • line / poem(45)
  • line(42)
  • chapter / book(41)
  • verse / chapter(38)
  • section / chapter / book(31)
  • line / book(26)
  • section / book(19)
Family 3 — Milestone-primary 698 documents  ·  18.5% of corpus

The primary citation unit is a <milestone> element. The <div> structure in these texts is vestigial or purely editorial; milestones carry the citation. This family is heavily concentrated in canonical-latinLit (296 documents, 43% of that corpus), where section and chapter milestones dominate the citation tradition.

216 documents in this family carry no effective citation declarations (cref_patterns = none). These cannot be migrated automatically; they require editorial assignment of a <citeStructure> before any transformation can proceed.

<citeStructure> for milestone-primary texts must match the milestone element directly: match="tei:milestone[@unit='card']". This differs semantically from the containment-based matching used in families 1 and 2: the returned element anchors a content range (everything between this milestone and the next of the same unit) rather than containing it. This distinction must be documented and reflected in how the XSLT chunkers handle the anchor.

Most common cRefPattern shapes:

  • (none)(216)
  • line(171)

  • section / chapter(54)
  • chapter(43)
  • section / chapter / book(13)
  • section / book(11)

Family 4 — Drama 77 documents  ·  2.0% of corpus

Dramatic texts (Greek tragedy and comedy, Roman drama) are encoded with a flat or near-flat sequence of <div type="textpart"> elements bearing many distinct one-off @subtype values: Prologue, Episode, Parodos, Agon, Exodos, strophe, antistrophe, ephymnion, and so on. Aristophanes exhibits the most complex subtype vocabulary; Sophocles and Euripides are somewhat more regular.

Notably, all 77 dramatic documents share the same cRefPattern shape: line. The dramatic structural divisions are encoding metadata but are not part of the CTS citation scheme; line number is the canonical citation unit throughout. This means family 4 does not require a specialized <citeStructure> for citation purposes — a simple line-level declaration suffices.

The structural complexity of drama is relevant to chunking, not citation. The StrategySelector already handles this via the flat-drama collapse: when a div type carries more than five distinct subtypes, all instances are treated as a single chunk type.

Family 5 — Parallel tradition 289 documents  ·  7.7% of corpus

These texts have a primary div-based citation hierarchy and a secondary scholarly milestone tradition representing an incommensurable citation system: Bekker pages in Aristotle, Stephanus pages in Plato, Jebb pages in Sophocles, Casaubon pages in Athenaeus, and similar conventions. The two systems cannot be unified into a single <citeStructure>.

The correct encoding requires two <refsDecl> elements, one with default="true" for the primary CTS citation and one with default="false" for the parallel tradition. ReferenceParser selects the default refsDecl; external consumers can request the alternative by refsDecl id.

The CSEL corpus contributes 76 documents to this family (28% of that corpus), a notably high rate. These are patristic texts that carry both a modern structural division and a traditional chapter/section or column citation system inherited from print editions.

Most common cRefPattern shapes (primary tradition):

  • section(177)
  • section / book(40)
  • section / chapter(16)
  • chapter(11)
  • chapter / book(11)
  • verse / chapter(11)
Family 6 — Unknown / other 97 documents  ·  2.6% of corpus

97 documents did not match any of the five defined families. 78 are in canonical-latinLit with no citation declarations; 19 are in csel with the pattern paragraph as the sole cref unit — an unusual declaration that may indicate non-standard encoding.

The absence of unknown documents in canonical-greekLit and first1kgreek (0 each) confirms that the classifier was calibrated adequately for the Greek tradition. The concentration of unknowns in latinLit reflects that corpus's lower editorial attention and higher rate of encoding irregularity.

4. Migration Planning

Migration converts each TEI source document from its current encoding (EpiDoc conventions, cRefPattern-based refsDecls, element-level @xml:base) to a Perseus-specific schema with <citeStructure>-based citation declarations and the CTS base URN on <body @xml:base>. The core transformation is handled by transform1.xsl; genre- and text-specific companions handle remaining structural idiosyncrasies.

4.1 Stylesheet requirements by family

Family Documents Migration stylesheet Effort
1. Hierarchical div 2,002 transform1.xsl covers book/chapter/section; additional patterns straightforward Low
2. Verse with <l> leaf 611 transform1.xsl + explicit <l>-level in generated <citeStructure> Low
3. Milestone-primary 698 Companion stylesheet for milestone-based <citeStructure>; 216 documents need editorial attention first Medium
4. Drama 77 transform1.xsl + simple line-level <citeStructure>; no special chunking changes needed Low
5. Parallel tradition 289 transform1.xsl + second <refsDecl default="false"> for parallel milestones; text-specific (fix_milestones_and_subchapters.xsl etc.) Medium
6. Unknown / other 97 Manual inspection required before any automated migration High

4.2 Editorial debt: documents without citation declarations

Approximately 300 documents cannot be migrated automatically because they carry no effective citation metadata. These fall into two groups:

  • 216 documents in family 3 (milestone-primary) with no cRefPattern or citeStructure at all
  • 78 documents in family 6 (latinLit) with no citation declarations

For each of these, an editor must inspect the document, determine the appropriate citation structure, and author a <citeStructure> declaration before transformation can proceed. This is editorial work, not engineering work, and represents the primary non-automatable cost of the migration.

4.3 latinLit as a special case

canonical-latinLit exhibits a substantially different profile from the Greek corpora: 43% milestone-primary (vs. 21% in greekLit), 11% unknown (vs. 0%), and 45 documents excluded entirely due to XML parse failures. The corpus has received the least editorial attention over the project's history and shows correspondingly higher rates of encoding irregularity. A dedicated audit pass targeting latinLit — separate from the Greek migration effort — is advisable before committing to a migration timeline for that corpus.

4.4 Recommended sequencing

The following order minimizes risk while delivering the largest automatable coverage early:

  1. Families 1 and 4 (greekLit and first1kgreek first): transform1.xsl covers most of these; test coverage is high.
  2. Family 2 (verse): extend transform1.xsl or add a thin companion to emit the <l>-level <citeStructure>.
  3. Family 5 (parallel tradition, Greek texts): requires text-specific companion stylesheets; begin with well-understood texts (Aristotle, Plato, Sophocles).
  4. Family 3 (milestone-primary, Greek and first1kgreek): after editorial review of the 216 undeclared texts.
  5. latinLit corpora (families 1–5): after a dedicated audit pass.
  6. Family 6 and remaining parse-error documents: after manual inspection.

4.5 Classifier notes

The current classifier has one known misclassification: 42 texts with Jebb_page as their primary cref pattern are placed in family 1 but belong in family 5. Adding Jebb_page to the PARALLEL_TRADITION_MILESTONES set in classify_corpus.py will correct this in a subsequent run. Other Sophocles-specific scholarly page conventions may require similar additions.
